# Beyond Automation: Evaluating Traceable Privacy Threat Modeling with LLMs

## Abstract

LINDDUN Pro offers a structured, systematic methodology for privacy threat modeling. An analyst must reason about every dataflow, process, and data store in a system's Data Flow Diagram (DFD) against seven privacy threat categories and their underlying threat trees. Large language models (LLMs) offer obvious leverage, and recent tools demonstrate that LLM-assisted privacy threat elicitation is feasible [1][2]. PILLAR [1] automates LINDDUN GO and Pro from a natural-language system description via multi-agent deliberation across several commercial LLMs, without retrieval grounding. PriMod4AI [2] instead grounds LINDDUN reasoning in a retrieval-augmented, structured knowledge base built from a DFD's JSON representation, and extends coverage to AI-specific, model-centric privacy risks that PriMod4AI itself argues PILLAR's general-purpose, not privacy-specific, retrieval approach misses. Yet recent surveys of LLM-assisted threat modeling find the field still exploratory, lacking standardized benchmarks and relying heavily on qualitative, expert-based evaluation [3].

We argue that trustworthy AI assistance in privacy engineering depends on traceability, and critically, on traceability that is *verified* rather than merely asserted. Our work develops and evaluates an approach in which every AI-generated threat cites two sources simultaneously: the specific LINDDUN Pro threat-tree node it instantiates, and the location in the system's DFD where it arises. Critically, both citations are independently re-derived against the knowledge base after generation, rather than trusted from the model's own output. This post-generation verification layer is the central enhancement we build on top of PriMod4AI, the closest prior system: PriMod4AI retrieval-grounds LINDDUN reasoning but still treats the model's self-reported citation as ground truth, whereas we re-check each citation against the knowledge base so that a fabricated node, an inapplicable threat type, or a nonexistent DFD location is caught deterministically rather than accepted (PILLAR performs no retrieval grounding at all).

The approach centers on a curated knowledge base encoding the LINDDUN Pro methodology as independently retrievable units: all seven threat types with the official LINDDUN threat trees (v241203, 65 nodes), and the mapping table governing which threats apply at each DFD interaction. Per-interaction methodology context is assembled by deterministic exact-match lookup against this mapping table — this is the proposed mechanism, since where an exact mapping exists, retrieval adds failure modes without adding information. A genuine retrieval-augmented (RAG) condition is retained for two reasons: it reproduces the architecture of retrieval-based prior work [2] inside our own pipeline, making the comparison controlled rather than cross-system, and it is the fallback for methodologies that lack a mapping table. Unlike PriMod4AI's DFD-only input and PILLAR's free-text-only input, our pipeline accepts a structured DFD, a source-code repository, or a DFD image, all converging on the same canonical DFD representation before entering the shared elicitation pipeline.

Whether grounded threats are actually correct must be demonstrated, not assumed. We evaluate on six scenarios with explicit ground-truth provenance: a children's video-streaming platform with a human-authored gold standard of 41 threats, used to develop and tune the pipeline; four author-constructed scenarios (family location-sharing, smart-home security, a school grades portal, and wearable fitness and health tracking) whose gold standards await independent review and are reported separately; and the NIST SP 1800-43C genomic-data catalog [4], evaluated under a second framework, MITRE PANOPTIC (below). All conditions run three commercial models (gpt-5.4, gpt-4o-mini, grok-4.3) through a single Azure AI Foundry endpoint, with structured output enforced via tool calls, one generation call per DFD flow, and sampling temperature pinned at 0. The core ablation is replicated three times per cell (45 runs) and reported as mean and standard deviation. The central quantitative result: deterministic grounding yields verified citation validity of **1.00 (sd 0.00)** on all five LINDDUN scenarios — not one fabricated tree node, inapplicable threat type, or nonexistent DFD location in 515 generated threats — against **0.87–0.98** for the RAG ablation and **0.82–0.84** ungrounded. Treating each scenario as a block, all three pairwise contrasts are significant and consistent in direction on 5 of 5 scenarios: grounded over ungrounded +0.170 (p<0.0001), grounded over RAG +0.068 (p=0.02), RAG over ungrounded +0.102 (p=0.006). We report the RAG figure after removing a confound we found in our own prompt: a single illustrative node id in the instructions was priming citation depth, and deleting it improved RAG citation validity on all five scenarios (mean +0.10) while cutting fabricated deep-node citations threefold. Nearly all residual invalid citations are two invented nodes at the two places the official trees are shallower than their own pattern suggests — structural extrapolation that grounding eliminates because the exact lookup supplies each tree's real shape. Across fifteen further model × input conditions (three models; hand-authored, source-derived, and image-derived DFDs; two third-party diagram exports), grounded validity was 1.00 in all but two (0.99, 0.97) — each a single fabricated identifier, both from the smallest model, and both caught deterministically by the verifier rather than shipped. Grounding also improves recall — highest on 5 of 5 scenarios, +0.158 over ungrounded (p=0.011) and +0.230 over RAG (p=0.00003), reaching a perfect 1.00 on two scenarios — but it elicits the most candidate threats, so its precision is the lowest and **F1 cannot separate the three conditions at all**: the mean grounded-minus-ungrounded F1 difference is +0.000 (p=1.00), and no F1 contrast approaches significance. An evaluation reporting F1 as its headline would conceal both real effects, which is why we lead with recall and verified citation validity and treat F1 as uninformative for this comparison. The claim also survives input degradation: deriving the DFD from source code rather than receiving it hand-authored costs recall (0.76 to 0.56, reachability-adjusted) while citation validity stays 1.00; reading the DFD from an image costs at most 0.08 recall, whereas switching models moves recall by up to 0.22 — model choice matters roughly four times more than input modality. The core ablation is n=3 per cell with dispersion reported throughout; the model-and-modality conditions remain single runs, for which we report separately measured run-to-run variance (~0.05 recall; five repeated source derivations spanned 107–115 flows) and do not interpret orderings inside that band. We note that pinning temperature to 0 reduces but does not eliminate nondeterminism on this deployment — three identical calls agreed on 8 of 10 cited nodes — so replication remains necessary rather than optional. Because our gold standards are curated catalogs rather than exhaustive enumerations, all reported precision (0.19–0.40 grounded) is a conservative lower bound; manual adjudication of unmatched threats is planned validation, and no conclusion above depends on it. Against PILLAR, the measured contrast is architectural rather than a scoreboard: in a preliminary, non-matched comparison, 82% of the node identifiers in a PILLAR export resolve against the official threat trees while 18% of its citation fields carry prose or empty strings in place of identifiers — and none are verified post hoc; ours are drawn from a closed vocabulary and re-derived after generation.

This work contributes a design pattern — closed-vocabulary citation plus deterministic post-generation verification — and an evaluation methodology for trustworthy AI assistance in privacy engineering, with particular attention to Non-compliance and Unawareness, the categories our analysis and prior work agree are hardest to automate. Beyond LINDDUN, initial results exist for MITRE PANOPTIC on the genomic scenario (F1 0.08–0.19 under exact sub-activity identifier matching, a deliberately strict metric); a coarser category-level tier is future work. Clearly planned, and not presented as evidence: independent review of the four author-constructed gold standards, manual adjudication yielding a human-corrected precision, and an auto-updating knowledge base that periodically re-scans authoritative sources, addressing the static-knowledge limitation PriMod4AI itself identifies.
